OUR STORY

Connecting Hokkaido’s wood with the people who shape it.

Bringing the character of Hokkaido burl wood and the work of lesser-known makers to the people who will use it. Ezowood Hokkaido develops and presents products that connect materials and workshops, people and everyday life.

01 / LANDSCAPE

From a landscape to the palm of your hand.

What comes to mind when you think of Hokkaido? A forest of pale trunks, mountains beyond a town, a sky opening towards the sea. Perhaps it is a view from a journey, or a place you hope to visit one day. A familiar object can be enough to bring a distant landscape back to mind, in the middle of an ordinary day.

It need not be a large piece of furniture or something hung on a wall. A single pen has a place on a working desk or beside a small diary. We hope to share the value of Hokkaido’s wood through tools used every day, making a connection where a feeling for a place meets an ordinary movement.

Burl and landscape images are references. Actual material, product, and partner-workshop photography is being prepared.

02 / CONNECTIONS

Between the maker and the person who uses it.

There are workshops and craftspeople who observe and work with wood, bringing its character into useful objects. There are also people who have yet to discover that work. Photographs, words and scenes of use can convey what the name of a material alone cannot. For Ezowood Hokkaido, the work of a brand is to create a point of connection between the two.

Our role is to develop products with partner workshops and craftspeople, and introduce the stories behind them. The work of shaping the wood and the work of sharing it with the people who will use it each have their own place. Respecting those roles, we consider how a pen will enter everyday life. We hope a reason to reach for it will grow from an appreciation of the grain into an interest in the people who shape it.

Burl and landscape images are references. Actual material, product, and partner-workshop photography is being prepared.

03 / BEGINNING

A small tool, a way into the material.

Our first product concept is a click-action ballpoint with a birch burl barrel. We want one small tool to offer a way to discover Hokkaido’s burl wood. Looking at the patterns gathered within burl and the areas that open into quieter space, the place where the eye rests can gradually change, even across a single pen.

We want to introduce the character of Hokkaido’s birch burl and other burl woods through more than a name or a description of rarity: how to look at them, and how they might find a place in daily life. To do so, we hope to connect the perspective of a workshop handling the material with that of the person using it. Our first pen is an introduction to the place and materials the brand values.

Burl and landscape images are references. Actual material, product, and partner-workshop photography is being prepared.

04 / NEW CONNECTIONS

Connections to a place, and to its makers.

For someone who has visited Hokkaido, a continuation of a journey. For someone drawn to Japanese making, a tool to bring into daily life. And for someone who does not yet know the region, a small reason to remember its name. Talking about the background of a pen may begin a conversation about a place or a material.

Introducing lesser-known makers, sharing the appeal of Hokkaido’s wood, and considering how we might take part in the future of its forests. Our ideas for supporting craftspeople and forest conservation are still to be developed. We want to begin by opening opportunities to discover materials and people, then share what we have actually done, one step at a time, in a form that can be checked.

THINGS TO KNOW

Things to know

Look into the background of a tool.

When you find a grain you like, let your interest extend to the kind of wood and the person who shapes it. A pen first chosen for its appearance can become an opportunity to learn about a place and a craft.

Write a little of a journey.

Alongside the name of a landscape, try leaving a line about what you saw there: the forest through a window, a town where you stopped, a road towards the sea. Rereading a small note can bring your own Hokkaido into clearer focus.
