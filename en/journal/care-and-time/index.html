CARE / 06

Small habits for continued use.

A wipe before putting it away, a regular place on the desk, somewhere to keep the instructions. To bring a tool received from its maker into your own days, here are small habits for life with a wooden writing instrument.

EZOWOOD JOURNAL / 06

In this story

01 — Let care follow putting things away.02 — Take another look at where it rests.03 — Keep the information you need.

Let care follow putting things away.

When you have finished, close the paper and return the pen. Make room within that movement for a gentle wipe with a soft, dry cloth. For some, a short moment then and there is easier to maintain than saving care for another day. Find a place for it within your own habits where it does not become a burden.

Look at the wooden surface in good light and check for dirt or moisture. Applying something to add shine is not the only form of care. Knowing its usual state and treating it gently with a clean cloth is also time spent caring for a wooden tool.

Take another look at where it rests.

The easiest place to use something is not always the best place to keep it. If the pen tends to end up beside a drink or on a sunny windowsill, try moving it a little. Return it somewhere away from water and strong heat, where it is less likely to fall. You need not change the whole desk to reconsider a place for one pen.

When carrying it, think about avoiding hard knocks against keys and other solid objects. Returning it to a consistent place can also reduce time spent searching. Rather than leaving a pen unused simply to protect it, think of use and putting away as parts of the same everyday rhythm.

Keep the information you need.

When looking for a refill or checking a care method, something remembered clearly at purchase can have become less certain over time. Keep the handling instructions or the page with the necessary details in one place. Being able to check when needed can be more useful than trying to become an expert on a tool.

If you notice a change that concerns you, make a brief note of the date and where it appeared. Rather than forcing a part to test it, pause and record what you can see. Like daily care, knowing the condition of a pen can be a small habit that supports continued use.
