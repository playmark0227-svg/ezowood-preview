THE MAKING

From the hands of a workshop to the person who uses it.

Working with partner workshops and craftspeople, we develop and present products that share the appeal of Hokkaido’s wood. Ezowood Hokkaido connects the work of making with the work of introducing it to the people who will use it.

01 / FORM

Developing the idea. Sharing its story.

Which material should we look towards, and what kind of tool might introduce it? Our starting point is to consider both the workshops that bring a material into use and the everyday lives of its users. Moving between a first encounter with the wood’s character and a scene of writing with the pen, we shape what we want to share.

The wooden barrels are intended to be turned by partner workshops and craftspeople. Ezowood Hokkaido’s role is to develop ideas for materials and products, introduce their background, and create connections with users. Rather than treating these as the same work, we want to give the makers’ knowledge and decisions a place of their own.

Burl and landscape images are references. Actual material, product, and partner-workshop photography is being prepared.

02 / IN THE HAND

Bring the user’s perspective into the idea.

Lift it from the desk. Set your fingers in place. Extend the tip and write a line. Retract the tip when you have finished, and put it down again. Even these brief actions involve a holding position, an angle and a movement of the eye. A writing instrument is a tool in motion as well as an object at rest. We bring that perspective of use into our product planning.

The way people hold a pen, and the time they spend writing, differ. Are they writing inside a small diary space, or drawing lines on a large sheet? A workshop looks towards the material and form; the user towards the movements of a day. Putting both perspectives into words can introduce a product through more than the beauty of its wood.

03 / MATERIAL & CRAFT

A workshop’s view of the wood’s character.

Where does the eye settle on a piece of burl? Seen as a complete pen, what conveys that wood’s particular character? A person enjoying a complex pattern and a workshop handling the material are likely to notice different things. As a brand, we want to create opportunities for those perspectives to meet.

The meeting of wood and fittings, the full outline, the surface revealed when a pen is lifted. We also want to listen to how makers consider such details. Rather than simply listing the names of processes, sharing the reason behind a decision can offer a way to understand the work of bringing a material into use.

Burl and landscape images are references. Actual material, product, and partner-workshop photography is being prepared.

04 / MAKERS & STORIES

Opening a view onto lesser-known work.

Careful work and widely recognised work are not always the same thing. Discovering workshops and craftspeople we have yet to meet, listening to what they value, and finding ways to introduce them to users: seeking out and presenting makers is one of the activities we hope to develop.

Which workshops might we work with, and in what way? We intend to share individual introductions once the details have been checked and are ready to present. Rather than telling the whole story only in the brand’s voice, we want to make room for the makers’ own perspectives. We hope to build the ideas and records for that, little by little.

THINGS TO KNOW

Things to know

Observe the pen you use now.

Place a favourite pen on your desk and notice where you usually hold it: the position of your fingers, how its weight feels, when you use it. Looking at a familiar tool makes your criteria for a new one more specific.

See both the material and the tool.

After enjoying a close-up of the grain, step back a little and look at the pen’s outline. Move between the beauty of the material and its place as an everyday tool. You may find more questions you would like to ask the maker.

About the making stories and images

The current images are illustrative. We plan to photograph the actual product and replace them. Workshop names, makers, and specific details of the specifications and processes will be introduced as the information becomes ready.
