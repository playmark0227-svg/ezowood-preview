A THOUGHTFUL GIFT

A pen, where words begin.

A tool looks a little different when you are choosing it for someone else. Imagine not only the moment of giving, but their desk and the time they spend writing.

01 / THE PERSON

Think of the moments when they write.

Will it sit on a desk at work, travel with a diary, or be used for letters at home? Imagining when someone reaches for a pen helps a gift take a clearer shape. A diary glimpsed in an everyday conversation, or a brief note sent from a journey. Small memories of the person can offer clues to your choice.

Someone who likes wooden objects, takes an interest in Japanese making, or cares for the things they use each day. Rather than a broad age group or a job title, picture a moment that feels like them. Being able to imagine the pen in use offers another starting point for a gift, alongside a preference for how it looks.

02 / ONE OF A KIND IN EXPRESSION

Add a few words about why you chose it.

“I thought you would like the wood.” “It reminded me of that journey.” Telling someone why you thought of them can sometimes say more than an impressive account of the object. Without searching too hard for special words, try writing a brief version of the thought that came to you as you chose it.

Gifts are not only for celebrations. They can thank someone for years of support, send a thought to a person far away, or offer a few words as someone begins a new chapter. Perhaps each time the pen is used, the words spoken when it was given might return as well. We hope a wooden writing instrument can hold time like that, too.

03 / THE PRESENTATION

Share nature’s differences.

When giving something made from natural wood, the differences in colour and pattern become part of its story. Where will the recipient’s eye settle on their pen? What will they discover as they turn it in their hand? Leaving a little space that the giver cannot entirely determine is part of the interest in choosing a wooden tool.

There is no correct way to look at grain. One person may like a pale area; another may follow a dark line. Looking at the same pen together, you might point to different places. Being able to talk about the material in front of you, as well as its background, offers a small pleasure that continues after the gift is given.

04 / WORDS TO ACCOMPANY IT

Bring a distant place a little closer.

For someone who shares a memory of visiting Hokkaido, begin with a landscape. For someone who has never visited Japan, begin with the name of the wood or a conversation about writing tools. There is no need to explain a whole region at once. The small scale of a pen can give it an easy place in an everyday conversation.

Where might it find a place in the recipient’s life? A desk by a window, a well-used notebook, a shelf for letters. Choosing a gift is also a little time spent imagining someone else’s familiar surroundings. We hope that discovering Hokkaido’s burl wood, and the workshops that bring it into use, can become another reason for giving.

THINGS TO KNOW

Things to know

A letter begins with one line.

Rather than trying to write every feeling of thanks or celebration, begin with why you chose this pen. A few words on writing paper you have chosen yourself can connect the object to your relationship with the person.

Imagine one moment of giving.

Think beyond the day you hand it over to a day when the recipient will use it, and the choice becomes more tangible. Will it stay on a desk or travel with a diary? Give that difference as much thought as its appearance.
