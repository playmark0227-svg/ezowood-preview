OUR PURPOSE

The value of wood, and the connections to come.

Introducing Hokkaido’s burl wood and its lesser-known makers. Beyond that, we hope to become involved in supporting craftspeople and the future of forests. What we share here is the brand’s concept, still to be developed into concrete plans.

01 / PEOPLE

Towards makers we have yet to meet.

Work with materials takes place in settings that users may rarely see. How is wood observed, and what matters when giving it form? A photograph of the finished object need not be the only way to discover a craftsperson’s work. Ezowood Hokkaido is considering activities to meet lesser-known workshops and craftspeople, and introduce what they do.

First, we want to listen and understand their thinking about materials and work. We hope to check the information with each maker and find a way to convey their own perspective. How we might support craftspeople, including how introductions and product ideas could lead to work, is still at the stage of being developed. We see introducing workshops and craftspeople as a starting point for building relationships.

Burl and landscape images are references. Actual material, product, and partner-workshop photography is being prepared.

02 / MATERIAL

Hokkaido’s burl wood, in the language of daily life.

The interwoven lines and shadows of Hokkaido’s burl wood, including birch burl. We want its value to be accessible beyond those who already know the material: something to look at, learn about and enjoy as an everyday tool. Beginning with a birch burl pen, we see the brand’s role as bringing materials and users closer together.

Our idea for the future is to deepen the introductions to the history and character of materials, alongside the perspectives of workshops. Rather than stressing rarity, we want to share where to look and what might be interesting. Before simply arranging products for sale, we want to make a place that invites curiosity about the region and its materials. Through those steps, we will consider ways for the value of Hokkaido’s wood to reach different people.

Burl and landscape images are references. Actual material, product, and partner-workshop photography is being prepared.

03 / FOREST

Considering a relationship with the forest.

As a brand introducing wooden tools, we also want to look towards the forests beyond them. Involvement in forest conservation is an idea we intend to learn about and develop into practical possibilities. Which activities and partners would be appropriate? We want to consider our role by first understanding local circumstances and the aims of existing activities.

We are not at a stage of reporting donations, tree planting or other completed activities. When methods, partners and actions have been decided, we intend to explain their scope clearly. What practical steps might sit between buying wood and caring for a forest? We will approach that as a future plan, rather than making a connection through words alone.

04 / SHARING

Sharing each step, clearly.

We hope to offer opportunities to discover materials and makers not only to individual users, but also to businesses, shops and people involved in local activities. For business projects and corporate social responsibility, understanding who is involved and what is being done matters alongside a product’s appearance. Considering how to introduce and share that information is also part of our plans for the future.

For activities that do take place, we intend to keep reports that distinguish the purpose, the people involved, the timing and what was actually done. We want to keep concepts separate from completed actions, and make progress possible to follow through photographs and words. Our aim is to prepare, step by step, information that we can also explain to businesses and organisations.

THINGS TO KNOW

Things to know

Where we are now

Support for craftspeople and forest conservation are concepts whose methods and potential partners are still to be developed. They are not presented as completed activities or achievements. As plans become ready, we will explain them with their respective details clearly set out.

For businesses and organisations

We are also considering how projects that introduce materials and workshop work might connect with information about social activities. Specific business programmes and collaboration terms are unconfirmed. When an initiative takes shape, we aim to introduce it with a clear account of its content and scope.

Future reporting

If we carry out activities, our policy is to record their purpose, results and what we will consider next. We have no completed activity reports at present; we are beginning by sharing the concept.
